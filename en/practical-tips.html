Practical tips

A few things worth fixing before building software.

These steps work for 5 to 30 person companies. They do not require a new system, but they quickly reveal where the real operational problem is.

If manual work still remains high after these steps, it becomes much clearer what kind of internal software is worth building.

Practical tips

Give every request an ID

A simple REQUEST-2026-001 format is enough to stop work living only in subject lines and memory.

Use no more than 6 statuses

New, under review, waiting for reply, offer sent, closed, blocked. If more are needed, the process needs clarification first.

Assign one owner per item

Shared responsibility often becomes no responsibility. Each active item should have one current owner.

Do not automate a messy process

First define input, status and output. A bad process automated will only produce mistakes faster.

Use templates for recurring documents

If a document is produced several times a month, it should have a reviewed template and a required data list.

Review blocked items once a week

The blocked list often says more than a long report. It shows where a decision or system change is needed.

When is internal software worth considering?

If the same data is entered multiple times, if item status is unclear, or if daily operations depend on one person’s memory, a small internal system is worth planning.
