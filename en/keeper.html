Documents that belong together should stay together.

An invoice in Downloads, a contract in email, a photo on your phone and an insurer's letter in a separate PDF. That works while there are only a few documents. Then the real question becomes not whether you have the file, but where it is, what it belongs to and whether anything still needs to happen. Keeper is being built for that point.

WHY IS IT BEING BUILT?

You do not need more folders. You need less searching.

Most people do not have a formal “document management system”. They have a Downloads folder, a few directories on a computer, email attachments, phone photos and paper in a drawer. That is normal — until one matter suddenly needs five or six different documents.

Keeper is being designed so you do not have to remember those relationships yourself. Documents for an insurance claim, warranty case, property matter or small-business project can stay together while the original files remain available.

The idea is deliberately simple.

You should not have to remember which folder contains a file. It should be enough to remember what the matter was about.

HOW WOULD YOU USE IT?

From one document to one understandable matter.

Keeper is not meant to make you design the perfect folder structure first. The aim is to let you bring in the document, then make it easier to place, connect and follow.

01

Bring in the document.

A PDF, image, photo or another supported document goes into Keeper. You should not need to rename and sort everything by hand first.

02

Recognise what it is.

The aim is to understand more than a filename: the kind of document and the details that may matter later.

03

Connect what belongs together.

Letters, invoices, contracts, images and other related documents can be kept as parts of the same matter.

04

Help you see what comes next.

What needs attention? What are you waiting for? What expires soon? What still needs review? Keeper is being built to make those questions easier to answer.

PLANNED FOLDER ORGANISATION

The planned workflow includes Keeper suggesting a folder or matter when documents appear to belong together, and — with the user's approval — creating the structure that keeps those documents in one place. The goal is useful automation, not silently rearranging important files behind your back.

FOLDERS AND RELATIONSHIPS

The folder is only the start. The relationship is what matters.

A document can belong to more than one context. A repair invoice may relate to the vehicle, the claim and the insurer conversation at the same time. Keeper is being designed so you do not need duplicate files to express those relationships.

Within a matter, the related documents, people or organisations, deadlines and next actions can be seen together. That means a case lasting several months does not have to be reconstructed from memory every time you return to it.

SEARCH AND ASK

04

“Is there an invoice or document I still need to review?”

PRIVACY AND CONTROL

With important documents, trust cannot be a footnote.

01

Local-first by design.

Keeper's product direction is local-first: document handling is not meant to depend on hidden cloud uploads.

02

The original stays available.

Important information remains connected to its source so you can check what the document actually says.

03

Your documents are not training material.

The product direction explicitly excludes using user document content for model training.

04

Consequential actions remain your decision.

Keeper may suggest or prepare something, but an external action with consequences requires explicit user approval.
